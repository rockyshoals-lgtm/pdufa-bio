## 1. What each cohort *really* wants (the truth under the feature list)

**Retail investors — they want confidence, not data.**
The emotional job at 6:30am is *"is anything about to happen to a name I hold, and how worried should I be?"* — and *"don't make me feel stupid, and don't sell me a dream."* They're not trying to find edge; they're trying not to get wrecked by a binary they didn't understand, and not to miss a date on a stock they own. Every competitor either hypes them (MarketBeat's "Top 5 Stocks to Buy Now") or hands them a seductive fake number (CatalystAlert's "AI %"). **Nobody is calm and on their side.** What wins retail: plain-English ("high phosphate in dialysis patients," not "Oxylanthanum Carbonate for kidney disease"), one human sentence per catalyst, a watchlist that *tells them* when their date is near, and a visible refusal to hype. The product that says *"here are the facts, here's what usually happens, we won't pretend to know the outcome"* is the one a beginner trusts and checks daily.

**Active / options traders — they want edge and speed.**
The job is *"find an asymmetric setup and time it"* — and *"don't make me the exit liquidity."* They live in: implied move vs the cohort's history, IV-crush risk, the T-120 run-up *path* (not a single %), "did the date quietly move," AdCom timing, cash runway / dilution risk. They distrust black boxes but love clean, dense, fast data they can verify. Unusual Whales gives them flow but not catalyst context; FDATracker gives them AdCom but a 2010s UI; nobody gives them *catalyst-scoped* options + run-up + base-rates + Silent-Shift in one fast, trustworthy place. **That combination is yours alone** — lean into it, keep it dense and keyboard-fast, and never blur into a generic options terminal (that's both off-thesis and a worse product than UW).

**Institutions / fund analysts — they want a defensible source of record.**
The job is *"feed my model, monitor my universe, and be able to defend the data to my PM."* They don't care if it's pretty; they care about provenance per fact, coverage/completeness stats, a freshness SLA, an API/CSV, and *honesty about limitations* — because their career risk is being wrong due to sloppy data. This is the rarest thing in the category: every competitor projects false confidence. **You already mark your own misses** (validation badges, "price-only" labels, corrections, "we don't predict"). That radical transparency is exactly what a fund trusts. Give them `/coverage` stats + an API + versioned methodology and you become citable — and a citable source earns the backlinks that win the head terms. The institutional layer and the SEO flywheel are the same flywheel.

**The thread that unifies all three: trust.** Retail is sold hype, traders get black boxes, institutions get unsourced data. Every cohort is underserved on the same axis. That's not three problems — it's one wedge.

## 2. What sets us apart — the one-line thesis
**"The biotech catalyst source that shows its work."** Three proofs, none of which a competitor can copy without rebuilding their pipeline *and* admitting their model is theater:
1. **Every fact links to its primary source** (FDA / SEC / CT.gov) — provenance is a *feature*, shown in the brand colour, not fine print.
2. **We publish our misses** — validation badges, "price-only," corrections. Honesty as a moat.
3. **We refuse to fake a probability** — and we say *why*, loudly (`/why-no-approval-probability`). The AI-PoA players (CatalystAlert, BiopharmaWatch) trained the market to expect a number; we make the *absence* of that number the trust signal.

Make this thesis literally visible: the most confident, most colourful element on every card is the **"✓ Sourced — FDA / SEC"** chip. Competitors spend colour on hype (red/green everywhere); we spend it on *trust*. That is the brand.

## 3. The look — welcoming + crisp + biotech-native (see the rendered palette above)
**Recommendation: go light-first.** Your current dark-navy-terminal reads "for pros" and has the AA-contrast problems I flagged; "welcoming + crisp" is light, airy, high-contrast, generous whitespace. But don't go generic-finance-white like MarketBeat/CatalystAlert — **own a colour.**

- **Signature: a clinical teal (`#0E7C86`).** Teal/cyan reads *medical, sterile, trustworthy, calm* — biotech-native, and distinct from the sea of finance-blue and hype-green competitors. It becomes "the pdufa.bio colour."
- **Canvas `#F7FAFC`, ink `#0B1F2A`, slate `#51677A`, hairline `#E3EBF2`.** Crisp, cool, clinical. Tabular numerals for all data. Lots of whitespace. 12px card radius, 0.5px borders, flat (no gradients/shadows). Two font weights.
- **Reserve green/red for outcomes only** (`#15803D` approved, `#B42318` CRL), amber `#B25E09` for "estimated date." Everything else is neutral slate + teal. This is what keeps it *calm* — competitors are a terminal sea of red/green; you stay quiet until there's an actual outcome to report.
- **Trust wears the brand colour:** the "✓ Sourced" chip is teal-tinted (`#E6F6F7`) — the one place colour signals *verified*, not *hot*.
- **Density:** bordered rows, not big cards, for lists (crisp + scannable). One human sentence + one number lead each row; trader jargon (Vol rich, IV crush, ±exp) demoted to a secondary line or tap — so retail isn't scared off and traders still get it.
- **Keep a dark "pro" mode** for the gated dashboard only (traders like a dark terminal; the rendered dark strip above shows it works). Light public/marketing/SEO surface + optional dark app = welcoming to retail, comfortable for pros.

Net: light, clinical-teal, whitespace-rich, colour-restrained, with provenance as the one confident accent. Welcoming enough for a nervous first-timer, crisp enough that a fund analyst trusts it, distinct enough to be recognisable in a screenshot.
