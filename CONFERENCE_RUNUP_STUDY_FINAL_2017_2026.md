## Headline: there is no reliable conference run-up.

Across a decade and 1,401 presentations, the **median stock does nothing** into its conference.

| Window (vs price at D-1) | n | **median** | p25 | p75 | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30-day run-up | 1,401 | **−0.35%** | −10.57% | +13.50% | +5.41% |
| 20-day | 1,401 | +0.07% | −8.18% | +11.26% | +3.87% |
| 10-day | 1,401 | +0.52% | −5.30% | +7.26% | +2.91% |
| 5-day | 1,401 | −0.08% | −3.65% | +4.63% | +1.08% |
| **Event day** | 1,401 | **−0.48%** | −4.73% | +2.50% | −0.43% |
| **D-1 → D+5** | 1,401 | **−1.55%** | −9.02% | +3.24% | −1.63% |
| **D-1 → D+10** | 1,401 | **−1.92%** | −10.75% | +4.37% | −2.31% |

Only **49.5% of presenters were positive at all** over 30 days — a coin flip. And **the drift after the conference is consistently negative** (−0.5% on the day, −1.6% by D+5, −1.9% by D+10).

## The mean is a lie
**Mean +5.41% vs median −0.35%**, standard deviation **33.6%**. The average is manufactured entirely by a right tail:

| 30-day run-up | share of presenters |
|---|---|
| ≥ +50% | 6.3% (n=88) |
| **≥ +25%** | **15.6% (n=219)** |
| ≥ +10% | 29.1% (n=408) |
| ≥ 0% | 49.5% |

**One in six presenters runs ≥25%. Five in six do not.**

## 2020 was an anomaly — and it's probably where the myth came from

| Year | n | median 30-day run-up |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 30 | +2.46% |
| 2018 | 36 | +2.41% |
| 2019 | 66 | +3.24% |
| **2020** | **47** | **+17.27%** ← *the biotech bubble* |
| 2021 | 71 | +2.19% |
| 2022 | 167 | **−3.33%** |
| 2023 | 357 | **−2.86%** |
| 2024 | 371 | **−1.74%** |
| 2025 | 177 | +1.22% |
| 2026 | 79 | +4.32% |

**2020 produced a +17.3% median — 5× any other year.** That is the COVID-era biotech bubble, not a conference effect. **The run-up then went negative for three straight years (2022–24).** Anyone who "learned" the conference run-up trade from 2020–21 learned a bubble artifact.

## WHWK — the case study that proves the shape
**AACR 2026 (Apr 17–22):** D-30 $3.57 → **D-1 $4.23** → D+1 $4.23 → D+5 $4.03
- 30-day run-up **+18.5%** · **5-day run-up +24.8%** · event day **0.0%** · D-1→D+5 **−4.7%**

The entire move happened *before* the presentation, most of it in the final five days. On the day: nothing. After: it gave back. **WHWK is a textbook right-tail draw (19.6% of the retail-zone cohort ran ≥25%) — not evidence of a repeatable edge.**

## Limitations — print these on the page
1. **Anchor = the date the conference data was reported**, not the abstract/title-drop date. WHWK's move concentrating in the final 5 days suggests the true anchor may be *late*. A dual-anchor rebuild is the next upgrade.
2. **Selection bias:** the universe is "readouts presented at a conference," not "all presenters." Conditioned on the data being newsworthy.
3. **Presentation type mostly unspecified** — catalyst text rarely encodes oral/poster.
4. Per-conference cells with n<25 are noise-dominated.

## The publishable story
> **"Do biotech stocks run up into medical conferences? We checked 1,401 presentations over 10 years. Mostly, no."**
> - The median presenter: **flat** (−0.35% over 30 days).
> - Only **half** are positive at all.
> - The average is positive only because **1 in 6** moves >25%.
> - **2020 was a bubble** (+17.3%); 2022–24 were *negative*.
> - **Nano-caps did worst** (−9.84%).
> - Whatever move happens **fades** (−1.9% by D+10).

Original, honest, decade-long, and un-copyable. It is the strongest possible expression of *"facts, not edge claims"* — and the best argument for why pdufa.bio refuses to sell probability scores.
