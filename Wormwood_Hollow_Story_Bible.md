# WORMWOOD HOLLOW — Story Bible v0.2

*Working title: **WORMWOOD HOLLOW** (alts: Saltline · Grímhollow · The Rotbound · Old Gnaw)*
*Genre: **idle-horror hybrid** 2D RPG (cave expeditions + offline dread) · Tone: Ozark folk-gothic, dread over gore · Mythos: Ozark folklore (surface) + Norse binding-legend (deep)*
*Protagonist LOCKED: a child raised in the Hollow who grows up over the game. Loop LOCKED: idle-horror hybrid. (See §10, §12, §14.)*
*Evolves the EMBERTHREAD bible — keeps the near-omnipotent-but-limited menace and the "reset/loop" bones, re-skinned as Appalachian-Ozark survival horror.*

---

## 1. Logline

A blight is creeping up the hollows of the Arkansas Ozarks, and the old folk wards — salt on the sill, iron over the door — are the only thing that ever held it back. When the wards fail and the one road out washes away, you learn the truth the granny-women never spoke aloud: the thing the salt keeps out is real, it is old, it has a Norse name carved on a stone no preacher can read, and it has been patient for a thousand years. Salt runs out. It does not.

---

## 2. The pitch

*Resident Evil* structure (isolated place, an unknown menace, a hidden source that unifies every monster, story told through documents, brutal resource scarcity) wearing the skin of **real Ozark folklore**, with a **Norse cosmic-horror** engine underneath. The hook: **the superstitions are true, and they're the only thing keeping the world alive.** Every "quaint" folk belief — salting a threshold, hanging a horseshoe, a granny's whispered charm — is a load-bearing containment ritual for a bound god no one remembers binding.

**Core marketing line:** *"They salted the doorways for a reason."*

---

## 3. Tone & voice

Plainspoken Ozark gothic — King-James cadence, holler dialect, dread in the understatement. The horror is *quiet and certain*, never splatter. Cosmic scale delivered in a grandmother's flat voice.

**Cold open (grandmother, remembered):**
> Grandma salted the sills every night of her life and never once told us why. We thought it was for luck.
> It was for the thing under Bald Knob. And the salt was the only thing that ever kept it.

**Preacher's journal (found document):**
> The stone in the cave-mouth is older than the county. Older than the Cherokee. Older than Adam, by the look of the letters. They ain't Christian letters and they ain't Latin. A man from the university called them *runes*, and then he called them a warning, and then he didn't come back down the mountain.

**Níðgrímr — Old Gnaw — spoken from below (heard in dreams, in the well-water, under the floorboards):**
> "You salt your doors, child. I have all the time under the mountain. Salt runs out."
> "They bound me with borrowed words. Borrowed things get returned."
> "Every winter, the wards get thinner and the graves get shallower. I am very patient. I am the shape patience takes."

---

## 4. Setting — Wormwood Hollow

A holler settlement deep in the Boston Mountains of the Arkansas Ozarks: a church, a general store, a grist mill on a spring-fed creek, scattered cabins up the draws, an old cemetery, and above it all the limestone bluff of **Bald Knob** with its cave-mouth. The Ozarks' real karst geography — sinkholes, spring-fed creeks, and vast cave systems — is the map: the deeper you go, the older and worse it gets, down toward the bound thing at the bottom.

**The lock-in (why you can't leave — RE's mansion door):** a "hundred-year flood" takes out the low-water bridge and the one road; the creek runs *black*; the phones/radios die (the Rot fouls copper and current). The Hollow is sealed, and the wards are failing from the inside.

---

## 5. The surface layer — real Ozark folklore (drawn from Vance Randolph et al.)

All grounded in documented Ozark tradition, made *literally functional*:

- **Granny-women** (mountain midwives) and **power-doctors** — folk healers who use charms, prayers, and exorcism. In-game: your mentors, quest-givers, and the keepers of the wards.
- **Salt & iron** — salt across thresholds keeps out haints; iron and silver ward evil. In-game: your core survival resources and safe-room mechanic.
- **"Two halves of the same whole"** — Ozark folk-Christianity blends scripture and charm without contradiction. The town's faith and its hexcraft are one defense.
- **Yarb doctors** — herb healers; your crafting/medicine system (mullein, pokeweed, wormwood, bloodroot).
- **Doodlebuggers / witch-wigglers** — dowsers who find water (and, here, find the Rot's veins underground).
- **The cryptids as symptoms, not causes:** the **Gowrow** (20-ft tusked cave-wyrm from the 1897 *Arkansas Gazette* legend), the **spook lights** (Hornet/Joplin light), **haints** — reframed as manifestations of the Rot leaking through failing wards. The monsters the folklore names are all *one thing* underneath.

---

## 6. The deep layer — the Norse binding & Níðgrímr

- Circa 1000 CE (per the real Heavener/Oklahoma runestone legend), Norse voyagers came up the Arkansas River — or something older *wearing* them did — and **bound** a devouring worm-god in the caves under Bald Knob, cutting its true name and a binding-rune into the living rock.
- **Níðgrímr** ("the curse-hooded one") — a Níðhöggr-style root-gnawer, the same near-omnipotent Hooded End from the Emberthread draft, now chained beneath a mountain. It cannot act directly; it can only *seep* — through roots, water, rot, and dreams. Its power is near-total but its reach is the length of the failing wards.
- Over centuries the binding degraded into folk memory: the runes became "witch marks," the containment ritual became "superstition," the priesthood became granny-women who kept the forms without the meaning. **The town is a cargo-cult of its own salvation.**
- **The "almost" (its one crack):** the binding can be *re-cut*. The original master-rune lies at the bottom of the cave system. Reach it, and the thing can be re-bound or unmade — but the deeper you go, the stronger its seep, and the fewer of you remain.

---

## 7. The Rot — the unifying source (RE's virus/mold)

The blight is Níðgrímr's exhalation: a fungal, ergot-like corruption (historical Ozark note: ergot on grain caused convulsions, gangrene, and hallucinations — "St. Anthony's Fire" — and fed real witch-panics). It rises through the water table (**Wormwood** — Revelation 8:11, "the waters became wormwood, and many died of the waters").

- **Spread:** roots → springs → wells → livestock → people. A slow tide up the hollow.
- **Transformation:** the blighted become the "monsters" of folklore — haints, hog-men, the Gowrow-touched. Body-horror rooted in *people you knew*.
- **Pressure mechanic:** the Rot advances over time whether you act or not (see §11). The Hollow is a clock.

## 8. Resident Evil structural DNA (the survival-horror skeleton)

| RE pillar | Wormwood Hollow |
|---|---|
| Isolated location | The flooded-in Hollow + the descending cave system |
| The unknown menace | Old Gnaw / Níðgrímr — unseen for most of the game, felt everywhere |
| Hidden unifying source | The Rot, seeping from the bound worm; every "monster" is one cause |
| Safe rooms | Salted, iron-ringed ground — a church, a granny's warded cabin |
| Scarce core resource | Salt, iron, silver (your "ink ribbons" — you never have enough) |
| Healing/crafting | Yarb-doctor herbalism; folk recipes |
| Documents | Remedy-books, preacher's journal, settler diaries, the folklorist's field notes, runestone rubbings |
| Keys / gates | Charm-keys, rune-etched tokens, granny's knots, consecrated ground |
| Regional bosses | Beloved folk figures twisted by the Rot (§9) |
| The final source | The master-rune at the bottom of the caves |

## 9. The corrupted folk — regional bosses ("the Lords")

Each is a pillar of the community twisted by the Rot; each guards a fragment of the binding.

- **The Granny-Witch, Bledsoe** — the eldest ward-keeper, who chose to *feed* the thing rather than watch her kin die. Now something wears her apron.
- **Brother Ezekiel, the Blighted Preacher** — took the black water for baptism; preaches to a congregation of the standing dead in the drowned church.
- **The Miller** — the grist mill grinds something other than corn now; the Rot spreads on the flour.
- **The Gowrow** — the folk-cryptid made flesh, the worm's largest limb, denning in the cave-mouth.

## 10. Protagonist — LOCKED: the Hollow-born child

You play a **child born and raised in Wormwood Hollow**, taught the salt-ritual as chores and rhymes before they're old enough to know what it's for. The game *is their life* — you grow from child to ward-keeper to elder, and the horror deepens as your understanding does. "You were always part of this" isn't a twist here; it's the premise. Full life-stage design in §14.

## 11. Story ↔ mechanics map

- **Wards & safe rooms:** maintaining salt/iron lines is your save/heal loop; wards decay, so home is never permanently safe.
- **The Rot clock:** corruption spreads across the map over time (and offline, if we keep an idle layer — see §12). Fight it back, or lose ground you cleared.
- **Descent = escalation:** the cave system is the difficulty ladder; each depth is a boss tier ending at the master-rune (replaces the "Nine Realms" ladder with "Nine Depths" under Bald Knob).
- **Meta-progression (if retained):** "what you carry out of each descent" — knowledge of true-names, re-cut runes, folk charms — the Emberthread "kept heat," reframed.
- **Multiple endings:** *Re-bind* (salt the door for another century — the cycle continues), *Unmake* (destroy the binding and the thing, at a terrible cost to the Hollow), *Inherit* (become the new keeper/warden — the tragic mirror).

## 12. Genre — LOCKED: idle-horror hybrid ✅

**Decision made: path #2 below (idle-horror hybrid).** The Rot advancing while you're logged off, and the salt running out, make the idle mechanics *serve* the dread instead of fighting it — and it keeps the residual-income / low-babysitting upside that made idle attractive in the first place. Full loop spec in §14. The three paths considered, for the record:

1. **Full narrative survival-horror RPG** — a 2D top-down RE-like. Best horror, most authored content, premium one-time price. **Highest babysitting / lowest residual** (it's a finished game, not a passive engine).
2. **Idle-horror hybrid** — you run *expeditions* into the caves; between runs, wards decay and the Rot creeps **while you're away** (offline "anti-progress" becomes dread, not idle comfort). Keeps residual-income + offline hooks; genuinely novel. **Middle ground.**
3. **Atmospheric idle with horror skin** — closest to the original plan, least scary. Probably the weakest creatively.

My read: **#2 (idle-horror hybrid)** is the sweet spot — it keeps the passive-income and low-maintenance advantages that made you pick idle, while the horror premise (the Rot advancing while you're logged off; salt running out) makes the idle mechanics *serve* the dread instead of fighting it. But #1 is the "make it a proper horror RPG" purist's answer. This is the one call I need from you.

## 14. LOCKED DESIGN v0.3 — The Keeper's Life

**Protagonist:** a child born and raised in Wormwood Hollow, taught the salt-ritual as chores and rhymes before they're old enough to know what it's for. The game is their whole life.

**The spine = growing up.** Time (the idle currency) ages the child through life-stages, and each stage unlocks what they can *do*. This replaces a generic tier ladder with a coming-of-age arc where knowing more is scarier, not safer:

| Stage | Age | Can do | Horror lens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Child** | ~6–12 | Chores: lay salt, feed stock, learn rhymes/charms. Hide & run — cannot fight. | Dread through innocence — you obey rules you don't understand; *we* understand them |
| **Youth** | ~13–17 | Yarb-craft, iron-work, forage the near woods, first encounters. **Transgression:** cross the treeline. | Rebellion cracks the wards — *you* cause the first breach |
| **Grown** | adult | Full cave expeditions, ward-keeping, combat, the descent toward the master-rune | The weight of knowing; the resource war against the Rot |
| **Keeper** | elder | Teach the next child, re-cut runes, the deepest depths | You become the granny; the cycle closes in your hands |

**Prestige = generations (the masterstroke).** One playthrough is one keeper's life. When they age out or fall, they **pass the salt to the next child**, and the *lineage* keeps a fraction of what was earned — lore, re-cut runes, cleared ground, charms. Níðgrímr is patient across centuries; your bloodline answers with patience of its own. The Emberthread "kept heat" is reborn as **inherited memory down a family line of ward-keepers** — each generation starts a little stronger, knows a little more of the true names, holds the line a little longer.

**The idle-horror loop (offline = time = dread + harvest).** While you're away, seasons turn:
- Salt depletes; **wards decay**.
- The **Rot advances** up the hollow — you can return to ground you'd cleared, lost.
- The **child grows / the keeper ages** toward the next generation.
- But time also *gives*: herbs grow, iron smelts, the child studies the remedy-book. Offline "earnings" are **half harvest, half bad news** — the tension that makes idle serve horror.
- Come back too seldom and you'll find the Hollow smaller, colder, the graves shallower.

**The Rot clock:** a slow, map-wide corruption meter that ticks in real time (faster while offline than your ward-strength can hold). Beating it back is the core active session; descending far enough — across generations — to re-cut the master-rune is the meta-goal.

### The Opening — "The Salt Line" (prologue script)

> *Low lamplight. Granny's hands are folded over yours, tipping the tin.*
> **Granny:** "Not too much, now. A finger's width, all the way 'round. Doorsill first, then the sills, then the corners. Corners is where they get in."
> *You ask her what gets in.*
> *She doesn't answer. She never answers that.* She hums the little rhyme instead — the one that isn't quite a hymn and isn't quite a song — and waits till you hum it back. The salt goes down white against the black wood.
> **Granny:** "Good. Now you never sleep without it laid. Not one night. Not when I'm gone, not when you're grown — not one night in all your life. Promise me on it."
> *You're six years old. You promise. You think it's a game.*
>
> *Seasons turn. You get taller; the chores get longer. You learn which herbs draw a fever and which draw worse. You learn the horseshoe hangs points-up so the luck don't run out. You learn not to whistle past the old cemetery, and not to answer if something out past the treeline calls your name in your mother's voice.*
> *You learn every rule but the one that matters — the one Granny never says aloud:*
> **the salt is not for luck.**
>
> *And then comes the morning you wake before the lamp, and the white line across your doorsill is* ***scattered*** *— swept in a long smear toward the bed, like something leaned in close to watch you sleeping —*
> *and Granny goes quiet in a way that scares you worse than any shout,*
> *and picks the tin back up with hands that shake,*
> *and for the first time in your whole life,*
> *lays the salt in the daytime.*
