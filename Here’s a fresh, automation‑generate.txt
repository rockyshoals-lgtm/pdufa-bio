## 📌 1) **Order Flow Imbalance & Metaorder Impact**

**Research insights:**

* Order Flow Imbalance (OFI) — net signed buy/sell order book pressure — remains a core predictor of **short‑term price moves, volatility clustering, and liquidity states**. OFI can be extended via generalized/multi‑level imbalance metrics that account for deeper book levels. ([Emergent Mind][1])
* A unifying microstructure framework shows **square‑root impact scaling** of metaorders and persistent cross‑correlations between order flows and price changes, reinforcing the predictive role of advanced imbalance measures. ([arXiv][2])

**Actionable signals:**

* **Short‑interval OFI:** net buy/sell pressure over 1‑5 min windows as a *directional* microstructure signal.
* **Generalized imbalance profiles:** multi‑level book imbalance capturing deep liquidity pressure.
* **Imbalance decay/persistence metrics:** autocorrelation or decay profiles that differentiate routine flow vs regime shifts.

---

## 📌 2) **Dealer Gamma Exposure & Hedging Feedback**

**Empirical and theoretical evidence:**

* Dealer **Gamma Exposure (GEX)** — the aggregate option gamma from open interest — correlates with market volatility regimes: *positive GEX* stabilizes (dampens volatility) while *negative GEX* amplifies price moves. ([arXiv][3])
* A theoretical model shows **beta‑dependent gamma feedback loops** where market‑maker hedging dynamics nonlinearly amplify volatility, especially in *low‑beta stocks* — a relevant consideration for smaller biotech names with high leverage. ([arXiv][4])
* Practitioner analytics (GEX indices) also highlight that *0DTE and near‑dated volume* can dominate hedging flows, shifting dealer behavior. ([LuxAlgo][5])

**Actionable signals:**

* **GEX level and derivative:** net gamma exposure and its change as volatility regime indicators.
* **Gamma regime tagging:** positive vs negative gamma epochs to modulate trading bias.
* **0DTE hedging pressure indices:** short‑maturity option flows that mechanically drive delta hedging.

---

## 📌 3) **Short/Securities Lending Dynamics**

**Recent empirical work:**

* Transaction‑level data decompositions reveal that **informed short sellers often supply liquidity**, not just consume it. They trade in ways that can *forecast returns around news days* while affecting liquidity conditions. ([ScienceDirect][6])
* Intraday shorting flows contain predictable components that vary with liquidity, borrower concentration, and competition among short sellers. Short‑flow predictive power is stronger when borrow markets are competitive (lower borrow concentration). ([pbcsf.tsinghua.edu.cn][7])

**Actionable signals:**

* **Intraday short‑flow measures:** aggressive short volume at open/midday/close as *predictive directional pressure* ahead of catalysts.
* **Borrower concentration proxies:** market lending metrics that modulate the strength of short‑flow signals.

---

## 📌 4) **Dealer Inventory Risk & Options Liquidity**

**Recent mainstream research:**

* Dealers’ inventory risk (especially near‑the‑money options) negatively relates to option liquidity, particularly when dealers are net short; this risk interacts with volatility and hedging constraints. ([SSRN][8])

**Actionable signals:**

* **Inventory risk proxies:** spreads and depth in near‑ATM options tied to dealer inventory.
* **Spread asymmetry:** put vs call spreads as liquidity stress signals.

---

## 📌 5) **Auction Imbalance & Intraday Price Discovery**

**Academic structures:**

* Auction and order placement dynamics, including **auction imbalance measures**, have been shown to influence short-term price formation and return autocorrelations — particularly when informational asymmetry is high. ([fernuni-hagen.de][9])

**Actionable signals:**

* **Pre‑open/pre‑close imbalance measures:** net buy/sell imbalance during auctions as directional indicators.
* **Return autocorrelation shifts:** signals of informational vs liquidity trading via intraday autocorrelation patterns.

---

## 📌 6) **Volatility Term Structure & Implied Signals**

**Datasets & practitioner analytics:**

* Volatility surface, skew, and term structure — when read jointly with GEX and net flows — provide predictive context for directional risk sentiment and hedging demand. Skew asymmetries (e.g., PUT/ATM differences) and term structure steepness can signal demand for protection or directional optionality.

**Actionable signals:**

* **IV term spreads:** differences between near‑term and longer‑term implied vols.
* **Skew/volatility meta‑features:** risk reversals and wing tail steepness as indicators of hedging and directional positioning.

## 📌 7) **Information Leakage via Option Market Events**

**Empirical academic evidence:**

* New option strike introductions beyond prevailing ranges can carry *information about underlying fundamentals*, consistent with informed trading activity that may not be fully reflected in other variables. ([fma.org][10])

**Actionable signals:**

* **Anomalous strike introduction counts:** relative frequency of new out‑of‑range strikes as potent early asymmetric information signal.
* **Extreme order imbalances around strikes:** delta‑weighted imbalance as early leakage indicator ahead of catalysts.

# Snapshotable diff pipeline for ClinicalTrials.gov

**Sources you’ll use**

* **AACT** daily snapshots (full relational mirror; easy local Postgres). ([AACT Database][1])
* **ClinicalTrials.gov Record History** (the “History of Changes”/Record History tab). ([ClinicalTrials.gov][2])
* **cthist** (mass‑download historical versions for NCTs). ([GitHub][3])
* **Modern API v2** (for current record pulls & metadata like last updated). ([National Library of Medicine][4])

---

## 1) Version capture (what to store per version)

From each historical version (cthist) or from Record History pages, store these normalized fields per **NCT + version_timestamp**:

* `primary_outcome.name`, `primary_outcome.description`
* `outcome_type` (categorize as time‑to‑event / binary / continuous)
* `enrollment.actual`, `enrollment.anticipated`, `enrollment.target`
* `study_status` (overall status)
* `primary_completion_date`, `overall_completion_date`
* `allocation`, `masking`, `phase`
* `protocol_id` (pointer to sponsor protocol PDF/URL if present)

AACT mirrors all protocol/result data daily (great for joins and sanity checks); Record History gives the true change trail; cthist bulk‑pulls the historical versions. ([AACT Database][1])

---

## 2) Diff logic (how to emit signals)

* **Lexical diff** on `primary_outcome.name/description`.

  * Use a **normalized token set** (lowercase, stem, stopword‑drop) to reduce noise (e.g., “progression‑free survival (PFS)” vs “PFS”).
* **Semantic class mapping** for primary endpoints → one of:
  `{survival, response_rate, biomarker, scale, binary}`

  * Implement with light **SBERT** embeddings + curated rule lists; emit `Δprimary_endpoint_class` when class changes (e.g., *PFS → OS* or *continuous → binary*).
* **Design flips**: any change in `masking`, `allocation`, or `phase` → immediate binary emit (these are rare but material governance signals).
* **Numeric rules (enrollment)**:

  * `Δn% = (new_target − old_target) / old_target`
  * Flag **moderate** when `|Δn%| ≥ 10%`, **high** when `|Δn%| ≥ 25%`.
* **Date slippage (execution risk)**:

  * `days_slip = new_primary_completion_date − old_primary_completion_date`
  * **Noteworthy** ≥ 30d; **high‑priority** ≥ 90d.

(AACT provides easy access to dates/enrollment/status; Record History pinpoints when the change was posted; API v2 gives “last changed” anchors.) ([AACT Database][1])

## 3) Implementation notes

* **Ingestion**

  * Use **cthist** to download all historical versions per NCT (or run nightly against fresh AACT & backfill with cthist). Store **immutable `version_hash`** (e.g., SHA‑256 of canonicalized JSON) and **timestamps** (ClinicalTrials “submitted” vs “posted”). ([CRAN][5])
* **Storage**

  * Keep a per‑NCT ordered list of versions; compute **rolling diffs** (prev→curr).
* **Output**

  * Emit a compact **JSON patch** per change for ODIN, e.g.:

# QA + cadence to avoid registry noise (ClinicalTrials.gov)

## Why this matters (quick)

Registry “updates” often are typo fixes or formatting tweaks. If we trade on those, we waste capital. The goal is: capture only **substantive** last‑mile changes before readouts and keep an **audit‑grade trail**.

## Polling cadence

* **Daily baseline:**
  • Pull `Record History` for all tracked NCTs.
  • Reconcile against AACT daily snapshot.
* **High‑watch window:**
  • For trials **≤180 days** from expected readout: poll **hourly** for version changes (spikes in last‑minute edits happen here).

## Evidence quality tiers

* **Tier‑A (ship as “high confidence”):**
  Registry history **version archived** + **sponsor primary doc** (protocol PDF or SEC filing). Use both to confirm.
* **Tier‑B (flag, but caution):**
  ClinicalTrials.gov version change alone.
* **Tier‑C (ignore):**
  Third‑party summaries without primary source.

## “Is this real?” filters (diff rules)

Only emit if at least one is true:

* **Semantic class change** in **primary outcome** (e.g., swaps response rate → PFS; changes endpoint definition class).
* **Numeric threshold change** that alters interpretation (e.g., Δ sample size, event count, non‑inferiority margin).
* **Substantive token change**: >5‑token edit **and** semantic class shift in outcome or eligibility.
* **Operational flips**: masking, allocation, or **phase** flips (always material).

Ignore:

* Pure formatting, spelling, capitalization.
* “Submitted” date vs “Posted” delays without content change.

## Gating thresholds (defaults; tuneable)

Emit a **candidate** when:

* (A) Primary‑outcome **semantic class change**, **OR**
* (B) **|Δn%| ≥ 15%** within **120 days** of readout, **OR**
* (C) **date_slip ≥ 30 days** within **180 days** of readout, **OR**
* (D) Any masking/allocation/phase flip at any time.

Promote to **High‑Confidence** only when **Tier‑A** corroboration arrives.

## Auditability (make it tamper‑proof)

Store for every emitted event:

* Full **HTML/XML snapshot** (and **sha256**), plus parsed JSON.
* **Version‑to‑version diffs** (field‑level + token‑level with semantic tags).
* **Upstream evidence links** (Record History URL, protocol PDF/EDGAR item).
* **Timestamped** decision record: which gate fired, what changed, and why it passed.

## Minimal implementation sketch (slots you can drop into ODIN)

**Objects:**
`TrialWatch { nct_id, readout_eta, tier, poll_freq }`
`RegistryVersion { nct_id, version_id, fetched_at, sha256, html_path, parsed_json }`
`DiffEvent { nct_id, from_version, to_version, changes[], gates[], confidence, evidence[] }`

**Pseudocode (ingest + gate):**

```python
def should_emit(diff, trial):
    gates = []
    if diff.primary_outcome.semantic_class_changed: gates.append("A")
    if abs(diff.sample_size_pct_change) >= 15 and days_to(trial.readout_eta) <= 120: gates.append("B")
    if diff.primary_completion_date_slip_days >= 30 and days_to(trial.readout_eta) <= 180: gates.append("C")
    if diff.design_flip in {"masking","allocation","phase"}: gates.append("D")
    substantive = (diff.primary_outcome.token_delta > 5 and diff.primary_outcome.semantic_class_changed) or bool(gates)
    return substantive, gates

def confidence(evidence_bundle):
    return "HIGH" if evidence_bundle.has_registry_archive and evidence_bundle.has_sponsor_primary_doc else "LOW"

# Poller decides frequency
poll_freq = "hourly" if days_to(readout_eta) <= 180 else "daily"
```

* Token delta: simple whitespace token count + Jaccard; cache.
* Semantic class: rule‑based keywords for endpoint families (ORR/CR/PR vs PFS/DFS/OS vs safety vs PK/PD); allow override dictionary by indication.

# Three repeatable micro‑patterns around PDUFAs

**Context (plain English):**
PDUFA = the FDA’s decision deadline for a drug. Near these dates, tickers often show consistent, *short‑lived* behaviors. Below are three lightweight patterns you can test as “soft priors” to guide T‑1 entries and risk limits.

## 1) Pre‑PDUFA volume spike (T‑10 to T‑1)

* **What it is:** Elevated dollar‑volume and range expansion in the final ~10 trading days before the decision.
* **Why it matters:** Even neutral newsflow tends to concentrate positioning; *early* spikes (T‑10 to T‑6) often precede a second push as event day approaches.
* **Simple trigger:**

  * Rolling 20‑day median dollar‑volume = `med20`.
  * If `sum(dollar_vol[T-10:T-6]) / (5 * med20) ≥ 1.6` **and** `ATRp[T-10:T-6] ≥ 1.3 × ATRp[T-30:T-20]`, mark **Pre‑Spike = True**.
* **Tactics:** If **Pre‑Spike = True** by T‑6, favor **staggered entries** (e.g., 40% on T‑6, 30% on T‑4, 30% on T‑2) with *tight daily max loss* (e.g., 1.0–1.5× ticker’s 20‑day ATR%) and a **no‑hold** rule into binary if ODIN POA < your tier cutoff.

## 2) Post‑Advisory drift (AdCom → PDUFA gap)

* **What it is:** After an FDA advisory committee (AdCom), the stock frequently “drifts” in the direction implied by the vote until PDUFA—especially if the vote margin is decisive (≥70/30).
* **Why it matters:** Positioning recalibrates between the AdCom and the final decision; the drift can be tradable even when the event outcome is already consensus.
* **Simple trigger:**

  * If `had_adcom = True` and `adcom_vote_pct ≥ 0.70`, compute a **vote‑strength score** = `(adcom_vote_pct - 0.5) / 0.5`.
  * Require **clean tape**: higher lows on 3 of last 5 sessions and no >‑8% single‑day drawdown.
* **Tactics:** Bias **buy‑the‑dip** on red days in the AdCom→PDUFA window with **intraday stop = 0.8× ATR%** and **daily stop = 1.2× ATR%**; scale down if borrow is tight or IV is extreme.

## 3) Two‑week reversion (T+2 to T+10)

* **What it is:** In the two weeks *after* the decision, outsized moves (up or down) tend to partially mean‑revert once the first wave of positioning is unwound.
* **Why it matters:** Liquidity pockets and hedges unwind; even strong trends often “give back” a slice.
* **Simple trigger:**

  * If absolute move from close(T‑1) → close(T+1) ≥ **+/‑ 25%**, then look for a counter‑move setup when you get the **first inside day** or **close back inside Boll(20,2)**.
* **Tactics:** Counter‑trend probes with **half size**, time‑based exits (e.g., 3–5 sessions), and **take‑profit at 38–50% of the event gap**. Avoid if fresh catalysts within 10 trading days.
