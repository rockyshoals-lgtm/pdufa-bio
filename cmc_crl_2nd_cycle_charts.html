Do CMC-only FDA rejections come back? CMC-only CRLs, 2020–2026

A source-verified, named-case look at Complete Response Letters driven only by manufacturing / facility (CMC) problems — where the drug's efficacy and safety were not in question. pdufa.bio · data as of Jun 26 2026.

The pattern: when the FDA's only problem is the factory, not the molecule, the drug almost always comes back — usually on the next cycle. Of 11 verified CMC/facility-only CRLs from 2020–2026 that have reached a final decision, all 11 were ultimately approved — 7 on the very next review cycle (median ≈10 months). The catch is in the fine print below: this is a curated sample, most 2025–26 cases are still pending, and a "CMC" label can hide a deeper problem.

Why manufacturing rejections are the recoverable kind

FDA's own data (Sacks et al., JAMA 2014, n=151 first-cycle CRLs to new drugs): drugs rejected on safety recovered to approval far more often than those rejected on efficacy. Manufacturing sits with the recoverable kind — the molecule isn't the problem.
What CRLs are actually about (same FDA analysis): pure CMC/labeling is only ~15% of letters — but it's the most fixable slice.

The honest counterexamples

tab-cel / Ebvallo (Atara → Pierre Fabre). The cleanest cautionary tale: a CMC CRL in Jan 2025 → the facility issue was resolved → and then a second CRL in Jan 2026 rejected the drug on efficacy (the single-arm ALLELE study was deemed inadequate). ~18 months unapproved, Atara cut ~85% of staff and flagged going-concern. Lesson: a clean factory fix doesn't make an un-approvable molecule approvable — verify the CRL was only CMC.

Ycanth (Verrica). Genuinely CMC-only — but it took three cycles over ~3 years to clear. "Next cycle" is the mode, not a guarantee.
Method & honesty notes. Cohort = named, source-verified U.S. NDA/BLA CRLs (2020–2026) where the letter was driven only by manufacturing / third-party-facility / cGMP / Form-483 / fill-finish issues, with efficacy and safety not cited. Each case was checked against the FDA's published CRL letter text and company disclosures; four cases were re-classified out during review (insulin icodec, patritumab deruxtecan, odronextamab, glepaglutide, tab-cel's 2nd cycle were not CMC-only). This is not a random sample and not a base rate. Raw "CRL → approval" rates from the openFDA file are biased (older letters were published precisely because they later approved; 2024–26 letters are mostly still pending), so we rely on named precedent + published literature, not a computed percentage.

Sources: openFDA Complete Response Letter transparency corpus (full letter text) · Sacks LV et al., JAMA 2014;311(4):378–384 & Nat Rev Drug Discov 2014 (CRL reasons & recovery) · Avalere / Clarivate (CRL prevalence & eventual-approval) · FDA PDUFA goals (Class 1 = 2-mo, Class 2 = 6-mo review) · company press releases / SEC filings.
