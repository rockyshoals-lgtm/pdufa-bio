# 2. THE DECISION-TIMING PAGE — good work, and one thing to fix

**It honours the denominator caution I set on 08-12g**, which I want to say plainly because that caution is the reason the rest of the site is citable:

- Scoped explicitly: *"the 18 2026 FDA decisions in this archive **whose outcome and dates we have checked against a primary source**"*
- **Counts, not percentages** — zero `%` tokens on the page. 13/18 stated as counts avoids implying a rate over an uncertain universe.
- **Publishes every row** — goal date → actual date → delta for all 18. The reader can recount it.
- Arithmetic checks: 13 + 4 + 1 = 18 ✅

## 🟠 But the inclusion rule correlates with the outcome being measured

I recomputed independently from the API. All 2026 `Decided` rows carrying **both** a goal date and a decision date:

| Set | n | Before | On | After | Share "before" |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Page (primary-source checked) | **18** | 13 | 4 | 1 | **72%** |
| All 2026 decided with both dates | **24** | 13 | **10** | 1 | **54%** |

**All six excluded rows landed ON the goal date. Not one excluded row was "before."**

The "before" count (13) and "after" count (1) are identical in both sets — the entire difference is six on-date decisions.

**The likely mechanism is intuitive:** an early or surprising decision generates a press release, which generates the primary source. A routine, on-time approval often doesn't. So *"has a checked primary source"* is **not independent of** *"was it early"* — which is textbook selection bias, in the direction that makes the FDA look earlier than it is.

I could not confirm from the decision pages *why* those six are excluded, so I'm not calling the page wrong — it states its rule and shows its work, which is more than anyone else does. **But 72% vs 54% is a big enough swing that it needs one disclosure line**, in the same spirit as refusing to publish an approval rate over unverified outcomes:

> *"Decisions we could not check against a primary source are excluded. All six excluded 2026 decisions landed on the goal date, so this sample may over-represent early decisions."*

That sentence costs nothing and it's the difference between a statistic and a citable statistic.

**Minor:** across the 18 deltas the median is **−2.5 days** (9th = −3, 10th = −2). The page says *"2 days before."* Worth stating as 2.5, or say "between 2 and 3 days."
