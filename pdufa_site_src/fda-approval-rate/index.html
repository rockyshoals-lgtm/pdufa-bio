FDA’s own PDUFA performance reports
Why ours is higher. The two are not the same measure. Ours counts every review cycle and the FDA’s counts only the first, and a resubmission after a CRL is approved far more often than a first submission, so an all-cycle rate runs higher. Ours also counts a supplemental approval for a new use, which large drugmakers file often and usually win, the same as a first approval, and a CRL enters it only once the company or the FDA has made the letter public. Our universe is publicly-traded drugmakers, from small biotechs to the largest companies; the FDA’s figure covers all NDAs and BLAs.

We publish both numbers rather than only ours. If our figure had matched the FDA’s exactly, that would have been the suspicious result.
Sources: FDA PDUFA Performance Reports and Independent Evaluation of FDA’s First-Cycle Review Performance. Our figure: 1,148 FDA decisions on listed biotechs. The 2025-26 FDA leadership upheaval

FDA leadership has turned over at an unprecedented rate. Robert Califf departed in January 2025; Marty Makary served as Commissioner from 2025 until May 2026; Kyle Diamantas became Acting Commissioner in May 2026. The drug center (CDER) itself cycled through multiple acting directors in 2026, industry reporting described several CDER heads within a single year. For catalyst watchers this matters because review priorities, complete-response-letter posture, and timelines can shift with leadership. Leadership is volatile: always verify the current officials at fda.gov.

What it means for FDA-catalyst traders

Approval counts are only part of the picture. In our tracked PDUFA dataset, the share of decisions ending in approval (vs a Complete Response Letter) ran 89.7% in 2024, 88.9% in 2025 and 91.2% in 2026 so far, but note that many CRLs are never press-released, so the true approval rate is lower than any dataset that only counts announced CRLs. More useful than a single number is the decision-day reaction by company size: in our 1,148-decision run-up study (2020-26), median absolute move on the decision was about 9.1% for nano-caps, 4.1% micro, 3.6% small, 2.4% mid and 1.0% large. These are historical base rates, not predictions for any one drug.

FAQ

How many new drugs did the FDA approve in 2025?
The FDA's Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER) approved 46 novel drugs in 2025, down from 50 in 2024 and 55 in 2023.
Who is the FDA Commissioner in 2026?
As of mid-2026, Kyle Diamantas is Acting FDA Commissioner, following Marty Makary's departure in May 2026. Verify current leadership at fda.gov.
What year did the FDA approve the most drugs?
Among 2020-2025, 2023 had the most CDER novel approvals (55); 2022 had the fewest (37).
