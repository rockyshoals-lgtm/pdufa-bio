What presenting companies' shares did, across the whole study
Across 1,425 presentations at major medical meetings from 2017 to 2026, the median presenting company's stock moved −0.03% in the 30 trading days before the meeting (49.8% rose) and −1.59% in the 5 trading days after (39.4% rose). Small caps: +2.75% before (n=298); nano caps: −7.86% (n=108). The middle half of all presenters ranged from −10.41% to +13.58%. The study is anchored on the meeting start date, not on the abstract release; the true catalyst may be earlier. This measures what happened; it is not a forecast for any company. How do biotech stocks typically trade around ESMO?
In our study of 192 ESMO presentations (2017-2026), the median stock moved -1.96% over the 10 trading days before the presentation (38% of them rose), -0.34% on the day itself, and -1.29% over the 5 trading days after. The pattern across the whole study is a drift up into these meetings and down out of them, strongest in the smallest companies. That is a historical distribution for a cohort, not a prediction for any one company, and not investment advice.
Why do medical conference dates matter for biotech stocks?
Trial results are often presented first at medical meetings, so a company's announced presentation is a dated, public catalyst. We publish a study of how biotech stocks have historically traded into conference presentations on the research page; it is historical measurement, not a prediction, and none of this is investment advice.
