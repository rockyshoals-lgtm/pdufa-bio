Questions

What is a Complete Response Letter?

A Complete Response Letter (CRL) is the FDA telling a company its application cannot be approved in its present form and what must be resolved first. It is not a final rejection: companies routinely address the deficiencies and resubmit.

How many Complete Response Letters has the FDA published?

The FDA's transparency dataset holds 458 Complete Response Letters, from 2002 through 2026, published as the agency's own redacted letters.

Do drugs get approved after a Complete Response Letter?

Many do: 309 of the 458 published letters belong to applications the FDA later approved. That count is not an approval rate -- the FDA's first transparency releases were letters for products it had since approved, and most recent letters belong to resubmissions still under way, so the published set over-represents happy endings by construction.

Can a drug receive more than one Complete Response Letter?

Yes. 83 applications in the FDA's published set received more than one letter across successive review cycles.
