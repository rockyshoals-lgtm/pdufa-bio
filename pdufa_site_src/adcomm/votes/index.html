FDA advisory committee votes and what the FDA decided

Page updated November 27, 2026·next FDA decision: on the calendar·Data as of Nov 27, 2026 3:51 AM ET
Every FDA drug and biologic advisory committee vote on a specific application that we have read from a primary source since 2020: each voting question as the FDA worded it, the yes, no and abstain count, and the FDA's first action on that application afterwards, with whether it followed the committee's overall vote.
54 committee votes on a specific application, 68 voting questions. 49 of the counts are read from the FDA's own minutes, the rest from the sponsor's release. Where a meeting had one overall question (44), the committee voted favorably 30 times and unfavorably 14 times. Of the 34 that ended in an approval or a Complete Response Letter, the FDA's first action followed the overall vote 27 times; it approved after an unfavorable vote 2 times and issued a Complete Response Letter after a favorable vote 5 times. Counts of the cards below, not a prediction for any pending application.
Newest meeting first. Jump to 2025 · 2024 · 2023 · 2022 · 2021 · 2020. Download every question with its counts and links: adcomm-votes.csv. The full meeting calendar, with every Federal Register notice since 2020, is on the advisory committee page.

Questions

Does the FDA have to follow an advisory committee vote?
No. Advisory committees give the FDA independent advice, and the agency makes the final decision. On this page the FDA's first action followed the committee's overall vote in 27 of 34 cases that ended in an approval or a Complete Response Letter.
What does 'followed the vote' mean here?
We take the one question on overall benefit-risk, effectiveness and safety, or approvability, as the FDA worded it. More yes than no votes is a favorable vote; more no than yes is unfavorable. An approval follows a favorable vote and a Complete Response Letter follows an unfavorable one. Ties, withdrawals, pending applications and meetings without such a question are not counted either way. An approval can still be narrower than what the committee voted on; the card says so where it is.
Where do the vote counts come from?
From the FDA's own meeting minutes for 49 of the 54 meetings, quoted from the document linked on each card. Where the FDA's minutes could not be read, from the sponsor's own press release or SEC filing, and the card says so. A news report alone is never the source of a count.
Is this every advisory committee meeting?
No. It lists meetings that voted on a specific drug or biologic application and whose counts we have read from a primary source. General-topic meetings, device panels and meetings without a vote are on the advisory committee calendar instead.
