About this calendar

This page lists 111 FDA decision dates covering June 2026 to December 2026. 93 of them have been decided and are marked with the outcome. 1 passed its target date without a published decision. Each date comes from an FDA notice or a company filing, except 2 rows marked (est.), which give an estimated quarter with no source behind it and are not counted among the goal dates still ahead. Where the source gives only a month or a quarter we say so instead of inventing a day.

A PDUFA date is the FDA's target date to complete review of a drug application, set under the Prescription Drug User Fee Act. It is a goal, not a promise: the agency can act before the date, on it, or after it.

A standard review carries a 10-month goal from the FDA's acceptance of the application; a priority review carries a 6-month goal. If the sponsor submits a major amendment during review, the FDA can extend the goal date by 3 months.

The FDA does not publish an official, forward-looking PDUFA calendar. Each date comes from a company filing, press release or FDA notice, except a date marked (est.), and each row links its own page, which names its source or says plainly that it has none.

Of the 41 FDA decisions in 2026 on this site with both a sourced goal date and a sourced action date, 27 came before the goal date, 13 landed on it, and 1 came after. The largest early margin was 110 days (IBTROZI (taletrectinib) sNDA, Sep 16). Full record: FDA decision timing.

On or around the goal date the FDA typically either approves the application or issues a Complete Response Letter explaining what stands in the way of approval. Decided rows state the outcome in words and link the decision record.

Run-up context, measured from a uniform T-120 baseline. Across 1,667 FDA decisions since 2020, the median peak gain from 120 trading days out was 17.5%, while the median move from T-120 to the day before the decision was 2.4%. The gap between those two numbers is the point: most of the move happens before the decision and is usually given back. 96% of events have a full T-120 window; the rest are excluded rather than back-filled.
