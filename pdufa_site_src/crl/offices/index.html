Which FDA office signed each CRL

Page updated November 25, 2026·next FDA decision: on the calendar·Data as of Nov 27, 2026 8:57 PM ET

Of the 299 released letters on applications openFDA marks Approved, 295 name the FDA division or office that signed them in text this page can read. Of the 154 on applications it marks Unapproved, none does, and 149 print "(b) (4)" in the signature.

Counted from the 453 distinct Complete Response Letters in the FDA's CRL release (openFDA transparency/crl, last updated 2026-11-18; snapshot read 2026-11-25), the same letters as /crl. Each count reads the letter's own signature block, from its last "Sincerely" to its electronic signature page, and the line after that page's legend; where openFDA's approver_center field names a division or office the scan missed, that is counted too. Of all 453 letters, 403 name the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research and 15 the Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research. "(b) (4)" is the label for information withheld under exemption 4 of the Freedom of Information Act (trade secrets and confidential commercial or financial information); in these letters it stands where other letters print the signer, and on the electronic signature page the date and time still show. Where a letter names no division or office, this page cannot tell whether one was withheld or never printed. The split follows openFDA's status field, not the letter's date: Unapproved letters of every year name none. These are counts of what the letters print, not a reading of why.

Letters on Approved applications counted here as naming no division or office, because the scan of their signature is too garbled to read: NDA 213135, March 13, 2020; NDA 215783, December 17, 2025; NDA 220748, June 17, 2026; NDA 220381, NDA 220381/Original 2, July 29, 2026.

An office is the first "Office of" the block names, other than the Office of New Drugs when a narrower office is also named; a division is the first "Division of". Names are as printed, OCR misreads included ("Office of Drug Evaluation IT" and "Office of Drug Evaluation |" appear to be scans of II and I), so one office can appear under two names, and the FDA renamed its review offices in 2019 and 2020. The FDA releases letters selectively; a count here is what the release holds.
