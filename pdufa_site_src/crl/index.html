This is a database of the letters the FDA has released under its CRL transparency program, published through openFDA; the FDA releases letters selectively, so the set is not every CRL ever issued. This page lists every released letter with a hosted file (465 distinct letters from 467 records), newest first, each linking the original PDF on FDA's servers. openFDA types 453 of them as complete response letters; the other 12 are other actions the release also holds (tentative approvals, a refusal to file, a letter rescinding two earlier CRLs, provisional determinations) or carry no type; they are listed and marked in the Letter column but not counted as CRLs. 169 listed letters are from 2024 or later; the archive reaches back to 2002. Where a letter matches a decision this site tracked, the decision page (with the run-up chart and outcome) is linked beside it. This page states counts, never approval rates; the section below explains why a rate from this data would mislead.

What the FDA has released, and what it does and does not show

A complete response letter is the FDA's formal notice, at the end of a review cycle, that it will not approve an application in its current form. The letter lists the deficiencies. It is not a permanent rejection: the sponsor can resubmit, and the same application is often approved on a later cycle.

467 records in the FDA's release (openFDA transparency/crl, last updated 2026-11-18). openFDA labels 312 of them as letters to applications the FDA has since approved and 155 as letters to applications not approved as of the release. The FDA described its first batch (July 10, 2025) as decision letters “associated with since-approved applications”; later batches added letters for applications still unapproved.

Read the split by year before drawing anything from it. Nearly every letter dated 2023 or earlier is on a since-approved application, and nearly every letter from 2025 on is not. That is the shape of a release policy (archived letters for approved applications first, then everything from 2024 onward), not the shape of the odds. The corpus shows that an application which receives a CRL can be approved later, 312 times over; it cannot say how often that happens, and this site does not compute a rate from it.

Questions

What is a Complete Response Letter?
A Complete Response Letter (CRL) is the FDA's formal notice that it will not approve a drug application in its current form. It lists the deficiencies the sponsor must address; it is not a permanent rejection. Of the 467 records in the FDA's release, openFDA labels 312 as issued to applications the FDA has since approved.

How many FDA Complete Response Letters are published here?
467 records in the FDA's release (openFDA transparency/crl, last updated 2026-11-18): 465 distinct letters with a hosted PDF are listed, 1 record repeats another verbatim and 1 is still marked under review for release. openFDA types 453 of the listed letters as Complete Response Letters; the rest are other actions the release also holds or carry no type. 169 of the listed letters were issued in 2024 or later; the rest go back to 2002.

How many CRLs are later approved?
This corpus cannot say. openFDA labels 312 of the 467 released records as belonging to applications the FDA has since approved and 155 as not approved, but the split follows the FDA's release policy, not the odds: the agency released archived letters only for applications it had approved, then every letter from 2024 on. Nearly every letter dated 2023 or earlier is on an approved application and nearly every letter from 2025 on is not. Counts are stated here; a rate from this data would measure what the FDA chose to publish.
