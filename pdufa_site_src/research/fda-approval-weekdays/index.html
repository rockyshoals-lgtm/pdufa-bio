On what weekday FDA approvals are dated

Page updated November 27, 2026·next FDA decision: on the calendar·Data as of Nov 27, 2026 1:42 PM ET

Of the 283 original NDA and BLA approvals in Drugs@FDA (medical gases aside) from January 1, 2025 to November 23, 2026, 89 (31%) carry a Friday action date and none a Saturday or Sunday date. Of the 462 efficacy supplement approvals, 162 (35%) are Fridays and 3 fall on a weekend.

An action date is the date the FDA gives its decision in Drugs@FDA, its public database of approved drugs, read through openFDA (read 2026-11-23 to 2026-11-27, the latest months last). An NDA is a new drug application and a BLA a biologics license application; an efficacy supplement changes the approved use of a drug, such as a new indication, patient group or dose. These are counts of dates on past FDA records: they do not say when any pending application will be acted on.

Approvals dated in the days just before a read may not have reached openFDA by then.

2026 decisions with a weekend goal date

Of the 45 sourced 2026 decisions in our timing study, 13 had a goal date on a Saturday, Sunday or federal holiday. The FDA record is dated before the goal date in 11 of them (8 within 3 days before, 6 of those on the Friday just before), on the goal date in 1 and after it in 1. None of these goal dates fell on a federal holiday. The record dated after the goal date is an FDA web notice. The longer record: dates in the Orange Book, 1982 to October 30, 2026

For NDAs since 1982, the Orange Book, the FDA's list of approved drug products, gives a longer record. Of the 3,977 NDAs with an approval date on every product they list, from January 5, 1982 to October 30, 2026, the earliest date listed is a Friday for 1,283 (32%) and a Saturday or Sunday for 12.

These are the dates the Orange Book lists, not always the FDA's action date. For the 184 NDAs with an original approval in Drugs@FDA since 2025 that are also in the Orange Book, the Orange Book date is later than the Drugs@FDA action date for 2 (Cypsedo: May 29, 2026 and August 27, 2026; Simtriyo: July 24, 2026 and October 30, 2026). The Orange Book lists products, not applications: 185 of the 3,977 NDAs are dated by a product other than product 001 (165 of them list no product 001), and an NDA whose prescription products have left the list (after a switch to over-the-counter sale, for example) is dated by a later product. 1,574 NDAs with any product listed as "Approved Prior to Jan 1, 1982" are left out, 146 of them with other products that are dated. Insulins and other protein products approved as NDAs moved to biologics licenses in March 2020 and are no longer in the file, and BLAs never were. An NDA no longer listed in the file, for any reason, is not counted. The FDA files are dated 2026-11-13; the newest approval date in them is October 30, 2026.

The 12 NDAs whose earliest Orange Book date falls on a Saturday or Sunday, as the file prints them: 018582 PROCALAMINE, May 8, 1982 (Saturday); 019115 FENTANYL CITRATE, January 12, 1985 (Saturday); 019345 DEXTROSE 30% IN PLASTIC CONTAINER, January 26, 1985 (Saturday); 020632 MERIDIA, November 22, 1997 (Saturday); 020934 LUXIQ, February 28, 1999 (Sunday); 021527 ATROVENT HFA, November 27, 2004 (Saturday); 021350 TRIGLIDE, May 7, 2005 (Saturday); 021807 SOLTAMOX, October 29, 2005 (Saturday); 022519 DUEXIS, April 23, 2011 (Saturday); 208510 VYVANSE, January 28, 2017 (Saturday); 210132 BIJUVA, October 28, 2018 (Sunday); 209472 PEMFEXY, February 8, 2020 (Saturday).

Method

Drugs@FDA: every original NDA or BLA (submission ORIG) and every efficacy supplement with status approved and an action date in the month, as openFDA returns them; generics (ANDAs) are out of scope, and one application and submission counts once. Orange Book: rows of the products file with application type N, grouped on the application number, each NDA taking the earliest approval date among its products. Weekdays are read from the date as printed. Holidays are the annual federal holidays of 5 U.S.C. 6103(a) at their observed dates (Martin Luther King Jr. Day from 1986, Juneteenth from 2021); Inauguration Day, which is a holiday only in the Washington area, and one-off closures by executive order, national days of mourning included, are not counted. An accelerated approval in the goal-date table is conditional: the FDA can withdraw it if the confirmatory trial does not verify the clinical benefit.
