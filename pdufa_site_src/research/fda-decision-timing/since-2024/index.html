Early, on the goal date, or late: FDA decisions since January 2024

Page updated November 27, 2026·next FDA decision: on the calendar·Data as of Nov 27, 2026 7:25 PM ET
Of 291 FDA decisions dated January 1, 2024 through Nov 24, 2026 whose goal date and action date we could each tie to a document, 173 came before the PDUFA goal date, 101 on it and 17 after it. The median decision came 1 day before the goal date.
Definitions. Goal date in effect: the PDUFA goal (target action) date that applied on the day the FDA acted, after any extension. Early: the FDA acted on a calendar day before that date. On the goal date: the same calendar day. Late: a calendar day after it. CRL: a complete response letter. Split: one action that approved part of an application and issued a CRL on another part the same day. Days are calendar days. A decision is counted in the year the FDA acted.

Goal dates on a weekend or holiday

70 of the 291 goal dates fell on a Saturday, Sunday or US federal holiday. Of those, 26 were decided on the last business day before the goal date and 1 on the first business day after it; overall 61 came early, 1 on the goal date itself and 8 late. 1 of the 17 late decisions came on the first business day after a weekend or holiday goal date (ONAPGO +2). Holidays here are the annual US federal holidays in statute; one-off closures by executive order are not treated as holidays. The FDA’s goal date can fall on a non-business day; an action on the business day before it counts here as early and an action on the business day after it counts as late, and each such row is flagged in the list below.

What is counted, and what is left out

For 2024 and 2025 the candidates were 334 PDUFA decisions on the pdufa.bio tracked list and 129 complete response letters the FDA has released that are dated in those years (463 in all). The tracked list is not every FDA action: it leans toward companies that publish their goal dates, mostly listed biotech and pharmaceutical companies. A decision is counted only when a sponsor or partner document dated before the action (a press release or an SEC filing), or the FDA’s own review of the application (the “PDUFA Goal Date” line on a review cover sheet posted on Drugs@FDA, or a CBER Summary Basis for Regulatory Action), states its goal date to the day, and an FDA record (Drugs@FDA, a CBER approval letter or summary, or the released CRL) or a sponsor document states the day the FDA acted. 2026 rows are the sourced decisions on the 2026 decision-timing page and follow its rules. Counted: 245 actions in 2024 and 2025 and 46 in 2026.

A separate review on November 26, 2026 re-checked 138 of the 2024 and 2025 actions against their documents (every one that was late, ended in a CRL, had an extension or rested on a search result, plus a random sample of the others) and the 2026 late and CRL rows. That review changed no goal or action date; it replaced some sources with the original filing, and the rows it left out are named below. Two further actions were changed to split decisions after the review, from the FDA’s own records. A third search pass later that day, through SEC full-text search, added 18 actions; a separate check read each one’s goal document and the FDA’s record of the action before it was counted. A fourth pass the same day read the FDA’s own review documents for the candidates still left out and added 41 actions (38 with the goal date from an FDA document); a separate check again read both dates at source for every one before it was counted. A fifth pass on November 27, 2026 read the other documents in each FDA approval package and the FDA’s later reviews of resubmitted applications, and added 14 actions (11 with the goal date from an FDA document); each was re-read at source before it was counted.

Questions

How many FDA decisions since 2024 came early, on the goal date, or late?
In this census of 291 sourced decisions dated January 1, 2024 through Nov 24, 2026, 173 came before the PDUFA goal date in effect, 101 on it and 17 after it. Each decision's goal date comes from a sponsor or partner document published before the action, or from the FDA's own review document posted with the approval, and its action date from an FDA record or a sponsor document that states the day. Some candidates were not yet researched, so this is not every FDA decision in the period.
Do late FDA decisions end in more complete response letters?
In the candidate sets not chosen by outcome (226 decisions), 0 of the 10 late decisions ended in a complete response letter (0%), against 18 of 142 early (13%) and 19 of 74 on the goal date (26%). The late group is small, so these are counts, not a measured effect. Across every CRL or split action in the census (62), including FDA-released letters, 25 came early, 35 on the goal date and 2 late.
How did the extended goal dates in this census turn out?
28 decisions in the census had a goal date the FDA extended before acting. Measured against the extended date, 16 came early, 8 on it and 4 late. Where the original date is sourced (21), 0 came before the original date, 1 on it and 20 after it.
Which decisions are left out?
Of 463 candidate rows for 2024 and 2025, 245 FDA actions are counted. Every other row is listed by reason on this page: no document stating the goal date to the day, a goal given only as a month or quarter, not a PDUFA action, not yet researched, the day the FDA acted not established, the same action listed twice, or left out at review. Nothing is estimated.
